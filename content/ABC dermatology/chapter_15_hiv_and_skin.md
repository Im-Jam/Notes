* **Identify the timing and morphology of the Seroconversion Rash.**
  **This is an acute exanthem that typically appears 2–6 weeks after exposure to the virus, presenting as a generalized maculopapular rash.**
  *   **Explanation:** This rash is part of the "Acute Retroviral Syndrome" (ARS), which resembles glandular fever (mononucleosis). The rash is the result of the body’s initial, massive immune response to the high viral load before antibodies are fully formed. It usually spares the face but covers the trunk and is often accompanied by fever and lymphadenopathy.

![[page444_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize the severity and distribution of Seborrhoeic Dermatitis in HIV.**
  **While common in the general population, this condition presents more severely in HIV-positive patients, often affecting the central face (nasolabial folds), scalp, and chest.**
  *   **Explanation:** This is often one of the earliest markers of immunosuppression. It is caused by an inflammatory reaction to *Malassezia* yeast. In HIV, the loss of T-cell regulation allows the yeast to flourish and the inflammation to become intense. Sudden onset of severe seb derm in a young person should always prompt an HIV test.

![[page446_img01.jpeg]]
* **Distinguish Eosinophilic Folliculitis from bacterial acne.**
  **This condition presents as intensely pruritic (itchy) follicular papules and pustules, typically found on the upper trunk, face, and neck.**
  *   **Explanation:** This is *not* a bacterial infection but a sterile inflammatory condition associated with advanced HIV (usually CD4 count < 200). The key clinical differentiator is the intense itching, which is usually absent in bacterial folliculitis. It represents immune dysregulation with eosinophils attacking the hair follicles.

![[page447_img01.jpeg]]
* **Understand the etiology of Nodular Prurigo.**
  **These are hyperkeratotic, firm nodules that develop secondary to a chronic "itch-scratch" cycle.**
  *   **Explanation:** In HIV, the skin becomes generally dry (xerosis) and prone to diverse inflammatory itches. The patient scratches repeatedly, causing the skin to thicken and nodulate. These are sometimes called "picker's nodules." Treatment requires stopping the itch (antihistamines/steroids) and breaking the habit.

![[page449_img01.jpeg]]
* **Identify the hallmark signs of Flexural Candida (Intertrigo).**
  **Look for beefy red plaques in skin folds (axilla, groin) with characteristic "satellite lesions" (small pustules or macules) outside the main border.**
  *   **Explanation:** Candida thrives in warm, moist environments. The "satellite lesions" are the crucial clue to distinguish this from tinea (ringworm) or psoriasis. In HIV patients, cell-mediated immunity is reduced, preventing the body from keeping normal skin flora like Candida in check.

![[page450_img01.jpeg]]
* **Differentiate Pseudomembranous Candida (Thrush) from other oral lesions.**
  **This presents as creamy white plaques on the tongue and buccal mucosa that can be scraped off, revealing a raw, erythematous base.**
  *   **Explanation:** This is a WHO Clinical Stage 3 condition. The ability to scrape it off distinguishes it from Oral Hairy Leukoplakia (which cannot be scraped off). It signals a falling CD4 count and usually requires systemic antifungal treatment (e.g., fluconazole) rather than just topical agents in immunocompromised hosts.

![[page451_img01.jpeg]]
* **Contrast Bacillary Angiomatosis with Kaposi's Sarcoma.**
  **These are bright red or purple vascular nodules caused by *Bartonella* bacteria, which can look identical to Kaposi's Sarcoma lesions.**
  *   **Explanation:** History is key: ask about exposure to cats (vectors for *Bartonella henselae*). A biopsy is required to differentiate this from KS; silver staining will reveal the bacteria. This condition is treated with antibiotics (erythromycin or doxycycline), whereas KS requires chemotherapy or ART.

![[page452_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize the classic presentation of Secondary Syphilis.**
  **This appears weeks to months after the initial chancre as a generalized, non-pruritic (non-itchy) rash that frequently involves the palms and soles.**
  *   **Explanation:** Syphilis is the "Great Imitator" and co-infection with HIV is common. The involvement of the palms and soles is a high-yield diagnostic clue. In HIV patients, syphilis can progress more rapidly to neurosyphilis, so aggressive treatment and monitoring are required.

![[page453_img01.jpeg]]
* **Understand the concept of Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS) in HSV.**
  **IRIS occurs when the immune system recovers (after starting ART) and mounts an exaggerated inflammatory response to a lingering infection, causing atypical or severe presentations.**
  *   **Explanation:** In image (b), the Herpes Simplex Virus lesion has become verrucous (wart-like) and hypertrophic. This is not because the virus is stronger, but because the recovering immune system is attacking the virus so aggressively that it causes massive tissue inflammation.

![[page454_img01.jpeg]]
* **Identify the specific location and cause of Oral Hairy Leukoplakia.**
  **These are corrugated, white, vertical ridges found specifically on the lateral borders (sides) of the tongue which cannot be scraped off.**
  *   **Explanation:** This is caused by the Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV). Unlike thrush, it is not fungal and is adherent to the mucosa. It is virtually pathognomonic for HIV infection and indicates significant immunosuppression, though the lesion itself is benign and painless.

![[page455_img01.jpeg]]
* **Perform a complete mucosal exam to detect Kaposi's Sarcoma.**
  **Always examine the hard palate, as this is a common site for the violaceous (purple/brown) non-blanching macules or plaques of KS.**
  *   **Explanation:** KS is an AIDS-defining illness caused by Human Herpesvirus 8 (HHV-8). It is a vascular tumor. Intraoral lesions can be subtle early on but can grow to obstruct the airway or cause bleeding.

![[page456_img01.jpeg]]
* **Characterize the cutaneous presentation of Kaposi's Sarcoma.**
  **Cutaneous KS presents as purple, brown, or reddish nodules that follow skin tension lines and may resemble ecchymosis (bruising) but are firm and palpable.**
  *   **Explanation:** In fair skin, they look purple; in darker skin, they appear dark brown or black. Unlike a bruise, they do not resolve over time. The primary treatment is initiating Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) to boost the immune system; chemotherapy is reserved for extensive visceral disease.

![[page457_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize atypical Molluscum Contagiosum in immunocompromised hosts.**
  **These are pearly, flesh-colored papules with central umbilication (a dimple in the middle), which can become giant, extensive, and affect the face in HIV patients.**
  *   **Explanation:** While common in children, extensive facial molluscum in an adult is a strong marker for HIV. The virus is a Poxvirus. Men typically spread it across the beard area through shaving (koebnerization).

![[page458_img01.jpeg]]
* **Assess the risk associated with extensive HPV Warts.**
  **These verrucous, cauliflower-like growths (condyloma acuminata) can be extensive and resistant to standard treatments in HIV-positive individuals.**
  *   **Explanation:** HIV patients have a higher persistence of High-Risk HPV types. Consequently, these warts carry a significantly higher risk of transforming into Squamous Cell Carcinoma (anal, cervical, or oral cancer). Regular screening (Pap smears/anal cytology) is crucial.

![[page459_img01.jpeg]]
* **Identify Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies.**
  **This presents as thick, hyperkeratotic, crusty plaques (often on hands and scalp) containing thousands to millions of mites.**
  *   **Explanation:** Unlike typical scabies which is intensely itchy, crusted scabies may not be itchy because the immunocompromised patient cannot mount the hypersensitivity reaction that causes the itch. Because of the massive mite load, it is highly contagious and requires oral Ivermectin plus topical permethrin to treat.

![[page460_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize the emergency of Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN).**
  **This is a life-threatening drug reaction causing widespread erythema, necrosis, and sheet-like detachment of the skin and mucous membranes.**
  *   **Explanation:** HIV patients have a specific metabolic inability to process certain drugs (glutathione deficiency), making them 100x more likely to react to Sulphonamides (like Co-trimoxazole used for PCP pneumonia prophylaxis). A positive "Nikolsky sign" (skin slips off when rubbed) is characteristic. Immediate discontinuation of the drug is required.
