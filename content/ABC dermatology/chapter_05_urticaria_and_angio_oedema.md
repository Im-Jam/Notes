*   **Distinguish this from ordinary hives by looking for residual bruising or purpura.** Ordinary urticaria fades without a trace; however, Urticarial Vasculitis involves inflammation of the blood vessel walls (leukocytoclastic vasculitis), leading to red blood cell leakage which manifests as bruising.
*   **Note that these lesions typically persist for more than 24 hours.** While standard urticarial weals are transient and move around, vasculitic lesions are fixed. **Tip:** Patients will often describe these lesions as "burning" or "painful" rather than intensely itchy.

![[page177_img01.jpeg]]
*   **Identify the classic "wheal and flare" reaction characterized by central swelling surrounded by an erythematous halo.** This is the hallmark of immediate hypersensitivity, driven by histamine release from mast cells causing superficial dermal edema (the wheal) and vasodilation (the flare).
*   **Observe the polycyclic (map-like) borders where individual lesions have coalesced.** **Physiological Point:** Despite the extensive appearance, individual weals in ordinary urticaria should disappear or move within 24 hours. If a single lesion stays in the exact same spot for >24 hours, suspect pathology other than simple urticaria.

![[page178_img01.jpeg]]
*   **Recognize this as Contact Urticaria, where the reaction is strictly localized to the area of external exposure.** Unlike ingested allergens that cause widespread hives, agents like caterpillar hairs (setae), latex, or nettles cause direct mast cell degranulation or immunologic reactions at the site of penetration.
*   **Look for a grouping of lesions that suggests a specific mechanism of injury.** The pattern often reflects how the caterpillar crawled or was brushed against. **Simplification:** Always ask about hobbies, gardening, or outdoor activities when hives are asymmetrical or localized.

![[page179_img01.jpeg]]
*   **Associate the location on exposed skin (cheeks) with environmental triggers like temperature.** This represents Physical Urticaria, specifically Cold Urticaria, where cooling of the skin triggers mast cell activation.
*   **Remember the potential systemic risks associated with this presentation.** **Extra Tip:** While a cold breeze causes hives, total body cooling (like jumping into a cold swimming pool) can cause massive histamine release leading to hypotension and drowning. Diagnosis is often confirmed via the "Ice Cube Test."

![[page181_img01.jpeg]]
*   **Identify the combination of linear streaks and widespread blotchy weals.** This indicates a patient with spontaneous urticaria who also exhibits the Koebner phenomenon (lesions appearing at sites of trauma/scratching).
*   **Understand the "Itch-Scratch" cycle in urticaria physiology.** The patient feels an itch (pruritus) from spontaneous hives, scratches the skin, and due to unstable mast cells, creates new linear hives (dermatographism) in the scratched areas.

![[page182_img01.jpeg]]
*   **Note the ring-shaped (annular) morphology with central clearing.** As the inflammatory fluid reabsorbs in the center, the active border continues to expand outward.
*   **Differentiate this from other annular rashes like Ringworm (Tinea) or Erythema Multiforme.** **Simplification:** The key is time. If the ring changes shape, moves, or disappears within hours, it is Urticaria. If the ring is scaly and fixed for days, it is likely fungal.

![[page184_img01.jpeg]]
*   **Contrast the deep, diffuse swelling seen here with the sharp, well-defined borders of urticaria.** Angio-oedema involves edema in the deep reticular dermis and subcutaneous tissues.
*   **Assess the patient for pain rather than itching.** Because the swelling is deeper and stretches the tissue rather than stimulating superficial nerve endings, angio-oedema is often painful or burning. **Clinical Pearl:** If angio-oedema occurs *without* hives, rule out ACE inhibitor use or Hereditary Angio-oedema (C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency).

![[page185_img01.jpeg]]
*   **Observe the distinct linear weal created by firm stroking of the skin ("Skin Writing").** This is Dermatographism, the most common form of physical urticaria, caused by shearing forces mechanically disturbing mast cells.
*   **Use this sign as a quick diagnostic tool during physical exams.** **Extra Tip:** You can reproduce this in the clinic by firmly scratching the patient's back with a tongue depressor; a positive result is the formation of a raised, itchy linear weal within minutes.
