* **Identify stable, chronic plaque psoriasis suitable for topical Dithranol.** This agent is an anthralin derivative typically reserved for "plaque" type psoriasis that is quiescent (stable) rather than inflamed or spreading rapidly.
    *   **Explanation:** Dithranol works by accumulating in the mitochondria and reducing cellular energy output, thereby slowing the rapid division of epidermal cells (antimitotic effect).
    *   **Tip:** It is famous for two side effects: burning normal skin and staining clothing/skin purple-brown. Students should recall "Short Contact Therapy" (applying for 10-30 mins then washing off) to maximize effect on plaques while minimizing irritation to surrounding skin.

![[page113_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize the distinct challenge of Scalp Psoriasis.** The presence of hair creates a barrier to topical creams and a reservoir for scale retention.
    *   **Explanation:** Treatment is biphasic. First, use keratolytics (like Salicylic acid or coconut oil compounds) to lift the thick scale ("descaling"). Second, apply active treatment (steroid lotions or coal tar shampoos) once the scale is removed so the drug can penetrate.
    *   **Tip:** Ointments are messy in hair; prescribe lotions, gels, or mousses for better patient compliance.

![[page115_img01.jpeg]]
* **Distinguish "thin" plaques for Narrowband UVB (TL01) therapy.** TL01 represents a specific wavelength (311nm ± 2nm) of ultraviolet B radiation.
    *   **Explanation:** UV light induces apoptosis in cutaneous T-cells and keratinocytes. However, UVB has limited penetration depth. If plaques are too thick (hyperkeratotic), the UV cannot penetrate to the basal layer to be effective.
    *   **Tip:** TL01 is safer and less carcinogenic than broad-spectrum UVB or PUVA, making it the first-line phototherapy choice.

![[page117_img01.jpeg]]
* **Understand the mechanism of PUVA (Psoralen + UVA).** This cabinet delivers Ultraviolet A radiation, which penetrates deeper into the dermis than UVB.
    *   **Explanation:** The patient ingests or bathes in Psoralen (a photosensitizing agent) before entering. Psoralen intercalates into DNA; upon UVA exposure, it creates cross-links that freeze DNA replication, halting cell proliferation.
    *   **Tip:** Because Psoralen remains in the lens of the eye, patients must wear UVA-blocking sunglasses for 12–24 hours post-treatment to prevent cataracts.

![[page119_img01.jpeg]]
* **Assess the baseline severity (PASI Score) before initiating phototherapy.** This image represents widespread guttate or plaque psoriasis that has failed topical therapy.
    *   **Explanation:** "Guttate" (raindrop) psoriasis often follows a streptococcal throat infection. Widespread coverage makes applying topical creams impractical, necessitating whole-body irradiation.
    *   **Tip:** Look for the *Koebner Phenomenon* in active cases like this, where new lesions appear along lines of trauma or scratching.

![[page121_img01.jpeg]]
* **Evaluate skin clearance and post-inflammatory changes.** This image demonstrates successful remission following a course of phototherapy.
    *   **Explanation:** While the scaling and induration (thickness) are gone, you will often see post-inflammatory hypopigmentation or hyperpigmentation. This is not active disease but a "shadow" of previous inflammation.
    *   **Tip:** Phototherapy is not a cure; it induces remission. Students should note that total lifetime UV exposure must be monitored to manage the risk of squamous cell carcinoma.

![[page123_img01.jpeg]]
* **Identify Palmoplantar Psoriasis requiring localized PUVA.** Psoriasis on the hands and feet is often resistant to topical steroids due to the thickness of the stratum corneum.
    *   **Explanation:** Hand/Foot PUVA uses high-intensity UVA on localized areas. Because the skin on palms/soles is optically thick, the deeper penetration of UVA (compared to UVB) is necessary to reach the immune cells.
    *   **Tip:** This condition has a disproportionately high impact on Quality of Life (DLQI) because it affects walking and manual dexterity.

![[page125_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize criteria for Systemic Therapy.** This patient exhibits widespread, confluent plaques that likely cover >10% of Body Surface Area (BSA).
    *   **Explanation:** When phototherapy fails or is impractical, oral systemics are introduced. The "big three" conventional systemics are Methotrexate (folate antagonist), Cyclosporine (calcineurin inhibitor), and Acitretin (vitamin A derivative).
    *   **Tip:** Students must memorize the monitoring requirements: Liver function for Methotrexate, Blood pressure/Kidney function for Cyclosporine, and pregnancy prevention (teratogenicity) for Acitretin.

![[page129_img01.jpeg]]
* **Identify Generalized Pustular Psoriasis (von Zumbusch) as a medical emergency.** This is an unstable form of psoriasis characterized by sheets of sterile pustules on an erythematous background.
    *   **Explanation:** This patient is likely systemically unwell (fever, electrolyte imbalance). "Biological therapies" (Monoclonal antibodies like Infliximab or Secukinumab) are indicated here because they target specific cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-17) to rapidly interrupt the inflammatory cascade.
    *   **Tip:** Never use systemic steroids (prednisone) to treat routine psoriasis, as withdrawing them can precipitate this severe pustular form.
