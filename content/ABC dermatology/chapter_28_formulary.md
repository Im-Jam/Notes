* **Identify the permanent structural damage known as Striae Distensae (stretch marks).**
  These linear, atrophic lesions are caused by the inhibition of fibroblast function and the subsequent reduction in collagen and elastin production. Unlike general skin thinning, striae represent dermal tearing and are largely irreversible, serving as a critical warning against the "liberal" or unmonitored use of high-potency steroids.
* **Recognize the risk factors regarding absorption and site.**
  This side effect is most common in intertriginous areas (skin folds like the groin or axilla) where occlusion occurs naturally, increasing systemic absorption and local potency. **Tip:** Always instruct patients to apply thin layers and avoid high-potency agents in areas where skin touches skin.

* **Diagnose Perioral Dermatitis by the location and morphology of the eruption.**
  Look for erythematous papules and pustules confined to the area around the mouth, often sparing a narrow zone immediately adjacent to the vermilion border (the lips). Physiologically, this is an acneiform reaction triggered by the use of fluorinated or potent steroids on the delicate facial skin.
* **Understand the "Rebound Phenomenon" and the difficulty of withdrawal.**
  Students must realize this condition creates a vicious cycle; stopping the steroid causes a severe flare-up, leading the patient to re-apply it for relief. **Simplification:** Treatment requires stopping the steroid (often slowly tapering to a lower potency) and potentially initiating oral or topical antibiotics (like tetracyclines or metronidazole), not adding more steroids.

* **Observe the signs of cutaneous atrophy (skin thinning).**
  The skin appears transparent, wrinkled, or "cigarette-paper" like, often revealing underlying blood vessels (telangiectasia). This occurs because corticosteroids have an antimitotic effect on epidermal cells and inhibit the synthesis of collagen and mucopolysaccharides in the dermis.
* **Distinguish Steroid Acne from Acne Vulgaris.**
  Note the presence of pustules in the image; steroid-induced acne typically presents as *monomorphic* (lesions all look the same stage) inflammatory papules or pustules. **Extra Tip:** Unlike typical teenage acne, steroid acne often lacks comedones (blackheads/whiteheads) and appears rapidly after the initiation of potent topical therapy.
