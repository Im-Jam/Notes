* **Identify the crucial anatomical landmarks: The Matrix, Nail Bed, and Nail Folds.**
    *   **Explanation:** The **Matrix** is the "root" where cells proliferate to create the nail plate; damage here causes permanent dystrophy. The **Nail Bed** lies beneath the plate, providing vascular support and adhesion (pink color), but does not produce the nail plate itself.

![[page578_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize the punctate depressions known as "pitting" on the nail surface.**
    *   **Explanation:** These pits are caused by parakeratotic cells (focal defects) in the **proximal** nail matrix that fall out as the nail grows. While highly characteristic of **Psoriasis**, pitting is nonspecific and also seen in Alopecia Areata and Eczema.

![[page580_img01.jpeg]]
* **Define Onycholysis as the distal separation of the nail plate from the nail bed.**
    *   **Explanation:** This creates a subungual space that appears white (due to air) and is prone to secondary bacterial or fungal infection. Mechanical leverage from aggressive manicuring (cleaning under nails) acts as a wedge, worsening the separation.

![[page581_img01.jpeg]]
* **Distinguish between simple onycholysis and psoriatic "oil drop" changes.**
    *   **Explanation:** In psoriasis, look for **Subungual Hyperkeratosis** (scale buildup lifting the nail) and the **"Oil Drop" or "Salmon Patch" sign** (yellow-red discoloration in the nail bed). This indicates active inflammation within the nail bed, distinguishing it from traumatic onycholysis.

![[page583_img01.jpeg]]
* **Identify the central longitudinal split often resembling an "inverted fir tree."**
    *   **Explanation:** This is **Median Canaliform Dystrophy**. It is typically a result of a temporary defect in the matrix, frequently exacerbated by "habit tic" trauma (picking at the proximal nail fold cuticle area).

![[page584_img01.jpeg]]
* **Look for the pathognomonic "V-shaped" nicking at the distal free edge.**
    *   **Explanation:** This is a hallmark of **Darier’s Disease**. It is accompanied by alternating longitudinal bands of red (erythronychia) and white (leukonychia), representing a fragility of the nail plate.

![[page587_img01.jpeg]]
* **Assess for the loss of Lovibond’s angle (the angle between the nail plate and proximal fold).**
    *   **Explanation:** In **Clubbing**, this angle exceeds 180°, and the "Schamroth's window" (diamond gap between opposing fingers) disappears. This is a critical sign of systemic hypoxia (lung cancer, cystic fibrosis) or inflammatory bowel disease.

![[page589_img01.jpeg]]
* **Differentiate "Apparent Leukonychia" from true leukonychia.**
    *   **Explanation:** Here, the whiteness is in the vascular **nail bed**, not the nail plate. A key diagnostic tip: if you apply pressure, the whiteness fades (blanches); if it were true leukonychia (in the plate), it would not blanch and would grow out with the nail.

![[page590_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize the triad of yellow, thickened, slow-growing nails with no cuticle.**
    *   **Explanation:** **Yellow Nail Syndrome** is associated with lymphedema and respiratory tract disorders (like pleural effusion). The loss of the cuticle and the "humped" transverse curvature are due to arrested longitudinal growth.

![[page591_img01.jpeg]]
* **Evaluate the longitudinal brown band for uniformity and width.**
    *   **Explanation:** **Linear Melanonychia** results from melanocyte activation or a nevus in the matrix. **Alert:** If the band is broad (>3mm), irregular, affects a single digit (especially the thumb/big toe), or involves the proximal nail fold (Hutchinson's sign), biopsy to rule out Melanoma.

![[page593_img01.jpeg]]
* **Link distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint arthritis with nail dystrophy.**
    *   **Explanation:** There is a very strong correlation between nail psoriasis and **Psoriatic Arthritis**. If a patient presents with nail pitting/onycholysis and a swollen DIP joint, it confirms the diagnosis.

![[page594_img01.jpeg]]
* **Observe the coarse, irregular transverse ridging and loss of cuticle.**
    *   **Explanation:** In **Chronic Eczema**, inflammation of the matrix creates "washboard" ripples in the nail plate. The loss of the cuticle (the seal) allows irritants to enter, perpetuating the inflammation (chronic paronychia).

![[page595_img01.jpeg]]
* **Identify thinning, longitudinal ridging, and dorsal pterygium.**
    *   **Explanation:** **Lichen Planus** of the nail causes matrix scarring. If the scarring is severe, the proximal nail fold fuses to the nail bed (**Dorsal Pterygium**), permanently destroying the nail plate (Anonychia).

![[page596_img01.jpeg]]
* **Differentiate Alopecia Areata pitting from Psoriasis pitting.**
    *   **Explanation:** Alopecia Areata typically presents with shallow, regular, geometric pits (grid-like pattern) or **Trachyonychia** (sandpaper roughness), whereas psoriasis pits are often deeper and more random.

![[page597_img01.jpeg]]
* **Check the proximal nail fold for erythema and dilated capillary loops.**
    *   **Explanation:** In connective tissue diseases like **Lupus (SLE)**, the primary pathology is often in the vasculature of the nail fold. These changes can serve as a barometer for systemic disease activity.

![[page598_img01.jpeg]]
* **Recognize scarring that obliterates the nail matrix.**
    *   **Explanation:** Similar to Lichen Planus, **Lupus** can cause scarring pterygium. This represents irreversible damage to the matrix; regrowth of a normal nail is impossible once this adhesion occurs.

![[page599_img01.jpeg]]
* **Identify the "bolstering" (swelling) of the proximal nail fold and absence of cuticle.**
    *   **Explanation:** **Chronic Paronychia** is inflammation of the fold, not the nail itself. It is usually caused by environmental exposure ("wet work") allowing Candida or bacteria to colonize the space where the cuticle should be.

![[page594_img01.jpeg]]
* **Distinguish between Superficial and Distal Subungual fungal patterns.**
    *   **Explanation:** **White Superficial Onychomycosis** appears as chalky white patches on the *surface* (top of plate), while **Distal Lateral Subungual Onychomycosis** involves thickening and debris *under* the nail plate.

![[page601_img01.jpeg]]
* **Diagnose the central "washboard" depression caused by mechanical manipulation.**
    *   **Explanation:** **Habit Tic Deformity** is caused by the patient habitually picking or rubbing the proximal nail fold (usually of the thumb) with the index finger. This constant trauma to the underlying matrix results in transverse ridges growing out down the center.

![[page603_img01.jpeg]]
* **Locate the translucent nodule near the DIP joint causing a nail groove.**
    *   **Explanation:** This is a **Myxoid (Mucoid) Cyst**, a focal herniation of joint fluid. It compresses the matrix, creating a longitudinal groove in the nail plate. Treatment involves addressing the cyst, not just the nail.

![[page605_img01.jpeg]]
* **Look for "Hutchinson’s Sign": pigment spreading onto the skin.**
    *   **Explanation:** In **Subungual Melanoma**, pigment extending from the nail plate onto the proximal or lateral nail fold (Hutchinson's sign) is a critical indicator of malignancy. Progressive widening of the band is also a red flag.

![[page606_img01.jpeg]]
* **Suspect this if a hard, painful nodule lifts the nail plate, especially on the big toe.**
    *   **Explanation:** **Subungual Exostosis** is a benign bony outgrowth (osteochondroma). It is distinguishable from a wart because it is rock-hard and requires an X-ray for diagnosis.
