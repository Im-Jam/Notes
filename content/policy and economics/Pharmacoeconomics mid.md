# Pharma Economics Quizzes & Study Materials

## Pharma Quizzes

**1. True or false:**
Efficiency is a key important concept in health economics to choose the intervention with the best values of money.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: True**
> **Explanation:** In health economics, **efficiency** refers to obtaining the greatest possible output (health outcomes) for a given set of inputs (resources/money). It is the core principle of "value for money."

**2. Heating bills within the hospital is considered \_\_\_\_\_\_ cost.**
*   a. direct overhead costs
*   b. Price
*   c. direct variable costs
*   d. direct capital costs
*   e. none of the above

> [!info]-
> **Answer: A**
> **Explanation:** Heating, lighting, and rent are considered **Direct Overhead (or Fixed) costs**. They are necessary to operate the facility but are not tied to a specific patient's treatment volume (unlike variable costs) and are not physical assets like machines (capital costs).

**3. True or false:** Pharmacoeconomics economics are concerned with the medical device products only.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: False**
> **Explanation:** Pharmacoeconomics evaluates pharmaceutical products (drugs) and services. However, the broader field of **Health Economics** evaluates all health interventions, including drugs, devices, surgeries, and public health programs.

**4. Costs of the child nursery as a result of illness are considered \_\_\_\_\_\_ costs.**
*   a. direct capital costs
*   b. indirect cost
*   c. direct overhead costs
*   d. none of the above
*   e. direct variable costs

> [!info]-
> **Answer: D**
> **Explanation:** The correct classification is **Direct Non-Medical Cost**. These are costs incurred by the patient to receive care (transportation, lodging, or childcare/nursery required during treatment). Since "Direct Non-Medical" is not listed in A, B, or C, the answer is "none of the above."

**5. Cost related to returning back early to work or being able to work for longer hours are considered \_\_\_\_\_\_.**
*   a. direct variable costs
*   b. direct capital costs
*   c. indirect cost
*   d. none of the above
*   e. direct overhead costs

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** **Indirect costs** (or indirect benefits/savings in this context) relate to productivity changes. Returning to work early represents a gain in productivity (or a reduction in productivity loss), which is an indirect component of the economic evaluation.

**6. True or false:** Variable costs are defined at those costs that are dependent of the level of production.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: True**
> **Explanation:** **Variable costs** change in proportion to the volume of output. For example, the more patients treated, the more syringes, drugs, and reagents are used.

**7. Paid hourly time of the clinician in the hospital outside their usual working days is considered \_\_\_\_\_\_ costs.**
*   a. direct overhead costs
*   b. none of the above
*   c. direct variable costs
*   d. Indirect cost
*   e. direct capital costs

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** While base salaries are often fixed costs, overtime or hourly pay for specific extra volume is considered a **Direct Variable cost** because it is incurred only when the additional service volume requires it.

**8. Fasting blood glucose test is considered \_\_\_\_\_\_ cost.**
*   a. direct variable costs
*   b. direct overhead costs
*   c. none of the above
*   d. direct capital costs
*   e. price

> [!info]-
> **Answer: A**
> **Explanation:** Each diagnostic test consumes specific resources (reagents, strips, technician time per test). Therefore, the total cost varies with the number of tests performed, making it a **Direct Variable cost**.

**9. True or false:** The term intangible cost is used in economics to refer to productivity and patient out of pocket losses related to illness or death.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: False**
> **Explanation:** **Intangible costs** refer to non-monetary factors like pain, suffering, and anxiety. Productivity losses are **Indirect costs**, and out-of-pocket expenses are **Direct costs**.

**10. The water used to reconstitute the paracetamol syrup is considered \_\_\_\_\_\_ cost.**
*   a. direct overhead costs
*   b. direct capital costs
*   c. none of the above
*   d. Indirect cost
*   e. direct variable costs

> [!info]-
> **Answer: E**
> **Explanation:** This is a raw material used to prepare a specific medication. The amount of water used depends on the amount of syrup prepared, making it a **Direct Variable cost**.

**11. True or false:** Costs for the accounting department within the hospital are direct medical cost.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: True (based on source)**
> **Explanation:** *Note: In strict accounting, this is often overhead/indirect.* However, in the context of this quiz, hospital administration and accounting are considered resources required to provide medical care, and are often allocated to the "bed cost" or general hospital operations, which falls under the broad umbrella of **Direct Medical Costs** (specifically overhead/fixed components of the medical provider).

**12. True or false:** Cost incurring is not depending on whether a patient is insured or not.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: False**
> **Explanation:** While the *resource consumption* (true economic cost) might be the same, the financial *cost incurred by the patient* (out-of-pocket) depends heavily on insurance. Furthermore, insurance status can influence the *utilization* of resources (moral hazard), changing the total costs incurred.

**13. Costs of the bed in the hospital are considered \_\_\_\_\_\_ costs.**
*   a. direct variable costs
*   b. Price
*   c. none of the above
*   d. direct capital costs
*   e. direct overhead costs

> [!info]-
> **Answer: D**
> **Explanation:** Furniture, equipment (like beds), and buildings are long-term assets. These are classified as **Direct Capital costs**.

**14. True or false:** Marginal costs equal average costs in public health comparison.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: False**
> **Explanation:** **Marginal cost** is the cost of producing *one extra* unit. **Average cost** is the total cost divided by the total units. They are rarely equal; usually, marginal cost decreases as volume increases (economies of scale) until capacity is reached.

**15. True or false:** Cost and Price are not interchangeable.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: True**
> **Explanation:** **Cost** is the value of resources consumed to produce a service. **Price** (or charge) is the amount asked by the provider/seller, which often includes profit or cost-shifting and does not necessarily reflect the true economic cost.

**Estimate extra cost per live saved comparing Drug A to B**
*   c. 16,700 per live saved

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** This is an Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio (ICER) calculation.
> $\text{ICER} = \frac{\Delta \text{Cost}}{\Delta \text{Effect}}$
> *   $\Delta \text{Cost} = \text{Cost B} (5M) - \text{Cost A} (4.5M) = 0.5 \text{ Million} (500,000)$.
> *   $\Delta \text{Effect} = \text{Lives B} (120) - \text{Lives A} (90) = 30 \text{ lives}$.
> *   $500,000 / 30 \approx 16,666$.
> Rounded, this is 16,700 per life saved.

**Graph Question (Threshold 1 vs 2)... Which of the following statements are/is correct?**
*   c. Threshold 2 represents higher affordability of a community for health interventions

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** In a Cost-Effectiveness Plane or threshold graph, a steeper slope (higher Y value for the same X) or a threshold line set higher indicates a higher **Willingness To Pay (WTP)** per unit of outcome. Threshold 2 allows for more expensive drugs to still be considered "cost-effective."

**Drug A (2000 JD) vs Drug B (2500 JD). Incremental QALY (B-A) is 0.25. What is incremental cost per QALY?**
*   b. 2000 JDs per QALY

> [!info]-
> **Answer: B**

## 015 Past Papers & Notes

**QUESTION 1: Which of the following statements represents the best definition of pharmacoeconomics?**
*   B. Process of identifying, measuring, and comparing the costs, risks, and benefits...

> [!info]-
> **Answer: B**
> **Explanation:** This is the standard comprehensive definition. It involves **Identification, Measurement, and Comparison** of both **Costs** and **Consequences** (outcomes) of pharmaceutical products/services.

**QUESTION 2: Which of the following best represents a direct medical cost and an indirect nonmedical cost, respectively?**
*   D. Medical professional time and lost productivity

> [!info]-
> **Answer: D**
> **Explanation:**
> *   **Medical professional time:** Direct Medical Cost (resource used to treat).
> *   **Lost productivity:** Indirect Cost (value of lost work/output).

**QUESTION 3: Which of the following statements is true regarding the perspective...**
*   C. Costs and consequences should be identified and measured relative to the perspective(s) selected

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** The "Perspective" (e.g., Payer, Patient, Society) dictates which costs are relevant. A robust analysis defines this upfront to ensure consistency.

**QUESTION 4: The appropriate use of cost-minimization analysis requires which of the following to be true?**
*   D. Consequences for each alternative must be equivalent

> [!info]-
> **Answer: D**
> **Explanation:** **Cost-Minimization Analysis (CMA)** can *only* be used when there is evidence (usually from trials) that the outcomes (efficacy and safety) of the comparators are essentially identical. The focus then shifts solely to finding the lowest cost.

**QUESTION 5: Which of the following are distinguishing characteristics of a "full" economic evaluation?**
*   D. A and C (Comparison of two or more treatment options AND Both costs and consequences are examined)

> [!info]-
> **Answer: D**
> **Explanation:** A **Full Economic Evaluation** must meet two criteria:
> 1.  Compare at least two alternatives.
> 2.  Measure both Costs and Consequences (Outcomes).
> (If only costs are measured, it's a Cost Analysis. If only outcomes, it's a Clinical Evaluation).

**QUESTION 6: Which of the following is not a common strategy for incorporating pharmacoeconomics into practice?**
*   B. Determining the cheapest alternative with respect to drug acquisition cost

> [!info]-
> **Answer: B**
> **Explanation:** Simply picking the drug with the lowest price tag (acquisition cost) ignores the outcomes and other associated costs (monitoring, side effects, hospitalization). This is "Cost Analysis" or procurement, not true **Pharmacoeconomics**.

**QUESTION 7: The economic evaluation method, cost-effectiveness analysis, would be best applied...**
*   C. When comparing two or more treatment alternatives that differ in clinical outcome

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** **CEA** is used when outcomes are measured in the same natural units (e.g., mmHg blood pressure reduced) but the *magnitude* of the outcome differs between treatments (and costs differ).

**QUESTION 8: Which of the following statements best describes "economic outcomes"?**
*   D. Cost savings associated with a disease or treatment alternative

> [!info]-
> **Answer: D**
> **Explanation:** Clinical outcomes are health changes; Humanistic outcomes are QoL/Satisfaction. **Economic outcomes** refer to financial impacts, such as costs avoided or savings generated.

**QUESTION 9: Which of the following statements... is false?**
*   B. Pharmacoeconomics should be the only consideration when making a drug therapy decision

> [!info]-
> **Answer: B**
> **Explanation:** Decisions should be based on the "ECHO" model: **Economic, Clinical, and Humanistic Outcomes**. Ethical considerations and clinical efficacy are paramount; economics is a support tool, not the sole dictator.

**QUESTION 10: When quantifying the value of a clinical pharmacy service... in a dollar value. Which economic evaluation method would you employ?**
*   A. Cost-benefit analysis

> [!info]-
> **Answer: A**
> **Explanation:** **Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA)** is the only method where the outcome is measured in **monetary units ($)**. This allows the calculation of a Net Benefit (Benefit $ - Cost $) or ROI.

**QUESTION 11: Which of the following statements provides the best description of an incremental cost effectiveness ratio?**
*   C. The extra cost to obtain an extra benefit realized from switching from one alternative to another

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** **ICER** represents the marginal cost for a marginal gain. It asks: "How much more do we have to pay to get this specific amount of extra health benefit?"

**QUESTION 12: Which of the following potential advantages could also be considered a potential disadvantage of using the literature...?**
*   C. A variety of results might be available for consideration

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** While having data is good, a "variety of results" can imply **inconsistency**. Different studies use different methods, perspectives, and patient populations, making it difficult to generalize or apply the "literature" to a specific local decision.

**QUESTION 13: Which of the following criteria are commonly used for evaluating the quality of a published pharmacoeconomic study?**
*   C. Evaluate the researchers' credentials...

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** While study design (B) and perspective (A) are technical checks, evaluating the **independence and credentials** of the authors is a standard part of critical appraisal checklists (like the Drummond checklist) to identify potential bias, though A and B are arguably more technical steps. (Note: The provided answer key selects C).

**QUESTION 14: Which of the following is true regarding the use of modeling...?**
*   C. Modeling is a relatively inexpensive method for obtaining pharmacoeconomic information specific to an organization or patient population

> [!info]-
> **Answer: C**
> **Explanation:** Conducting a real-world clinical trial (Piggyback PE) is very expensive and slow. **Modeling** (e.g., Markov models) allows researchers to simulate long-term outcomes using existing data, which is faster and cheaper.

**QUESTION 15: Which of the following is considered a disadvantage of conducting a local pharmacoeconomic evaluation...?**

> [!info]-
> **Answer: A**
> **Explanation:** Gathering primary data within your own hospital/organization yields the most relevant results, but it requires significant resources (staff time, data collection systems), making it **expensive and time-consuming**.

*   A. Cost-benefit ratio.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: A**
> **Explanation:** CBA results are typically presented as:
> 1.  **Net Benefit:** Benefit ($) - Cost ($).
> 2.  **Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR):** Benefit ($) / Cost ($).

> [!info]-
> **Answer: A**

The option says "the lost benefit was worth the extra cost" (implying the *previous/more expensive* drug was actually good value). If the lost benefit is very high value, switching to the cheap/bad drug is **not** cost-effective.

**22. The primary reason to perform a sensitivity analysis is to accomplish which one of the following?**
*   A. Test the robustness of the economic evaluation conclusions.

> [!info]-
> **Answer: A**
> **Explanation:** Pharmacoeconomic models rely on assumptions (estimates of cost, efficacy, discount rates). **Sensitivity Analysis** varies these assumptions to see if the final conclusion changes. If the result stays the same despite changes, the result is "robust."
